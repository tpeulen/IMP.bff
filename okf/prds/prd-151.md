# PRD-151: exact linear-Gaussian inference over the factor graph (aGrUM's CLG canonical forms)

**Status:** done (2026-09-17). **Done:** `include/InferenceCanonicalForm.h` (Gaussian factors in
canonical form), `include/InferenceGaussianElimination.h` (variable elimination over an
`InferenceFactorGraph`, with relevance pruning), the `"weighted"` heuristic of
`InferenceFactorGraph::get_elimination_order` (aGrUM's default triangulation), bound in
`pyext/include/IMP_bff.inferencegaussian.i`; tests and the aGrUM A/B fixture in `test/factorgraph/`.
ChiSurf's `core/fitting/canonical.py` is deleted and its engines condition through bff. **Missing:**
no bff or ChiSurf caller builds *per-factor* Gaussians yet, so the elimination runs in tests only (see
Open 1). **Next:** Open 1.

**Owner** (tpeulen, 2026-09-17): "agrum is bff domain, the algos." The graphical-model algorithms
harvested from aGrUM belong in bff; ChiSurf only consumes them. The lead was recorded in
`../chisurf/okf/references/agrum-mining.md`, which targeted ChiSurf and is now re-homed here. The CTBN
half landed first ([PRD-150](prd-150.md)).

**Author:** Claude Opus 5 for tpeulen.

**Placement.** The algebra is Eigen, and its consumers (`InferenceFactorGraph`, the Bayesian and fit
layers) are bff's, so the tttrlib rule for simple header-only algorithms ([PRD-146](prd-146.md)) does
not apply; the owner ruling above settles it anyway.

## What landed

- **`InferenceCanonicalForm`** -- `exp(g + h.x - x.K.x/2)` over a named scope; a variable may hold
  several numbers (its size, as in `InferenceFactorGraph`). aGrUM's operations under their meaning:
  `product`, `divide`, `extend` (augment), `reduce` (evidence out of scope ignored, as
  canonicalForm.py 386), `marginalize` (names what goes), `from_linear_gaussian` (`fromCLG`). What
  ChiSurf's `canonical.py` had and aGrUM lacks, ported: `from_moments` with a total mass,
  `get_log_normalizer` (mass survives marginalisation, so a posterior built with its evidence keeps
  reporting it), `get_log_density`, strict `condition`, `marginal(keep)` in the order asked, refusal of
  a repeated name.
- **Singular K, explicitly.** A singular precision is carried: it multiplies, conditions and
  marginalises. `get_is_proper`, `get_rank` and `get_null_space` (orthonormal basis of the unconstrained
  directions) describe it; only `get_mean`/`get_covariance` refuse, with the rank in the message, and
  `get_log_normalizer` is `+inf`. Integrating out a block that is not positive definite is refused as a
  divergent integral (aGrUM raises there too, but only through numpy's `inv`). Properness is decided on
  the Jacobi-scaled K, so an amplitude of 1e6 beside a lifetime of 1e-9 is proper.
- **`InferenceGaussianElimination`** over an `InferenceFactorGraph`: `set_factor` (a form may read no
  variable outside the factor's scope, sizes must agree), `set_evidence`, `get_posterior(targets,
  normalized, heuristic)` (aGrUM's `canonicalPosterior`, line-cited), `get_log_evidence`, and
  `get_relevant_factors`. **Added over aGrUM:** relevance pruning for a normalised posterior -- factors
  that reach no target except through observed variables are dropped before any algebra, which is exact,
  and a factor beyond the evidence need not even have a Gaussian.
- **`"weighted"` elimination order.** aGrUM's default triangulation, non-incremental; a clique's weight
  is the sum of its variables' sizes (the dimension a Gaussian step inverts), as aGrUM's is the sum of
  log domain sizes. Two findings from reading it: the quasi-simplicial phase is unreachable
  (`_updateList_` compares an integer division of two `Size`s with 0.99), and ties follow hash order, so
  bff breaks them on the flat-vector index.

## Checks (`test/factorgraph/test_inference_gaussian.py`, 41 tests)

- **A/B against aGrUM.** `test/factorgraph/agrum_clg_reference.py` transcribes `canonicalForm.py`
  verbatim, `CLG._build_canonical_forms`, `CLGVariableElimination` (including its in-place list
  mutation) and the default triangulation, without pyagrum and without IMP.bff, and records
  `data/clg_agrum_fixture.json`: random forms over overlapping scopes, three networks (a diamond with a
  moral 3-clique, a collider with explaining away, a chain), 10 queries each normalised and not, two
  singular cases, five random graphs for the order (two with unequal sizes).
  - form algebra (product, division, reduce, marginalize): max difference **2.2e-16**;
  - posteriors `(K, h, g)`, normalised and not: **2.5e-14**;
  - the weighted order equals aGrUM's on all 3 moral graphs and 5 random graphs.
- **Exact against brute force** (the joint of a linear-Gaussian BN is closed-form): posterior means
  **3.6e-14**, covariances **2.3e-13**, `log p(evidence)` **1.2e-14**. The textbook conditional and the
  sliced marginal of a random 5-d Gaussian to 1e-12; a conditioned form's mass equals the density of the
  held values.
- **Singular K:** a factor constraining only `a + b` has rank 1 and null space `(1, 1)/sqrt 2`;
  integrating `b` out gives K = 0 exactly as aGrUM, where `toGaussian` fails and bff's `get_mean` names
  rank 0; conditioning, or a proper prior, gives aGrUM's moments. `K_yy = 0` refuses to integrate.
- **Pruning:** with evidence on `S2` of a chain, the posterior of `S0` reads three factors and equals the
  unpruned computation to 1e-13; a graph where the far factors have no Gaussian still answers.

## ChiSurf consumes it

- `chisurf/core/fitting/canonical.py` is **deleted**, no shim. `GaussianEngine` holds an
  `IMP.bff.InferenceCanonicalForm` (built by one `_curvature_form` helper shared with `LaplaceEngine`);
  the conditional-scan plot reads its scope from it.
- **Conditioning as a closed-form query.** `LaplaceEngine.condition` used to re-fit per query. It now
  takes the Gaussian conditional from bff and **certifies** it with one Jacobian at the conditional
  mode: inside every bound, Newton step below 1e-3 conditional sd, curvature equal to the conditional
  precision to 1e-6. Certified, it is what a re-fit returns; otherwise it re-optimises and says why.
  `ProfileEngine` and `SamplingEngine` always re-optimise, since not assuming a Gaussian is their
  purpose. Every conditioned marginal carries `diagnostics["conditioning"]`. Measured on
  `c + a x + b x^2`: closed form against forced re-fit differs by 8e-9 sd in the value, 6e-9 relative in
  the sd, 6e-9 in the log evidence (10 model evaluations against 15 on this three-parameter fit); on a weak
  second exponential held 2 sd out the certificate refuses (Newton step 3.3 sd) and the engine re-fits.
- **A ChiSurf bug the certificate exposed, fixed:** `GlobalFitModel.parameter_values` derived its dirty
  set from the values it *found*, so a value written on a parameter object and then assigned in a vector
  looked unchanged and its dataset was skipped -- stale residuals, a Jacobian 1e11 too large. It now
  diffs against the vector the local models were last computed at. Guardrail
  `test_global_structure_aware_update.py::test_a_value_written_on_the_parameter_object_is_not_skipped`.
- ChiSurf's `FactorGraph` loses its Python triangulation for a caller-supplied order (no caller) and
  its `markov_graph`; every structural query is the engine's, and `elimination_order` accepts
  `"weighted"`.

## Open

1. **Per-factor Gaussians for a global fit.** `GaussianEngine` builds one dense form over every
   parameter. A global fit's Laplace approximation factorises by dataset (each local fit's curvature
   over its own scope, plus priors), and `InferenceGaussianElimination` would then condition and
   marginalise at the cost of the treewidth instead of the parameter count. Needs a per-local-fit
   curvature (the graph objective has the per-dataset residual blocks). Measurement: conditional query
   time against the dense form on a 20-dataset star, answers equal to 1e-10.
2. **Exact joint for multi-label rotamer weights.** `rotamer_mean_field_weights_multi_probe` treats
   labels on one structure as a pairwise MRF and solves it by mean field. For two labels the exact joint
   is one `n1 x n2` table; measure how far mean field is from it (weights, then `<R_DA>`/E) on a shipped
   two-label case before deciding anything. Only if it matters and three or more labels are common would
   discrete exact or loopy inference be needed -- and that is textbook, not a reason to keep aGrUM.
3. **A calibrated Gaussian junction tree** (all single-variable marginals in two passes, using
   `divide`) instead of one elimination per query. No consumer asks for many marginals of a factored
   posterior yet.
4. `junk/aGrUM` holds nothing further bff needs: the CLG package and the triangulation are harvested,
   the transcriptions and fixtures are self-contained, and the discrete core is rejected above. It can
   be deleted.
