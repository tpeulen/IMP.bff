# PRD-149: the lambda grid of the analytical route, in C++, as the Python prototype already does it

**Author:** Claude Opus 5 for tpeulen. **Written:** 2026-09-16 06:20 CEST.

**Owner** (tpeulen, ucfret prompts 474-476): "I already knew that the sampling would not work ... thus, i
went the more analytical route", "go with the lambda grid", "if you look at old prototypes, there was
already a lambda scan".

## Why

The analytical route is: the mode, the Laplace evidence with the spline integrated out, the roughness
weight `lambda` integrated over a grid (Rue, Martino & Chopin 2009), and delta-method bands. bff has the
grid (`bayesian_decay_fit_lambda_grid`, `BayesianEvidenceMixture`) and ucfret's `cbm56_best` runs it, but
only as an equally spaced scan with evidence weights. The Python prototype
(`ucfret/investigation/pinn_pR_anisotropy/s88_laplace_posterior.py`) already carries four things the C++
path does not, each of which was put there for a recorded reason:

| prototype | what it is | why (prototype's own note) |
|---|---|---|
| `LAM_NODES`, walked outward from 1 | 12 coarse nodes | warm starts from the neighbour |
| `LAM_PRIOR_SLOPE` (default 0) | the weights are `evidence + slope * log10 lambda` | "smooth unless the data insist" (prompts 269-270): a node one decade rougher must beat the smoother one by `slope` nats |
| `LAM_REFINE_STEP` 0.25, `LAM_REFINE_WINDOW` 10 nats | nodes added where the coarse evidence is within the window of its maximum, warm-started from the nearest fitted node | "p(lambda given D) looks too coarse" (prompt 272); INLA's exploration around the hyperparameter's mode |
| `mixture_summary_moments`, `SUMMARY_METHOD='tk'`, `TK_MIN_WEIGHT` 0.02 | the mixture as `sum_i w_i Phi((t - mu_i)/sd_i)` -- analytic, no draws -- with Tierney-Kadane moments for nodes above the weight floor | a mixture's sd is not the mean of the nodes' sds; TK corrects the delta method where the weight matters |

Without them the C++ result is a coarser grid with no tilt, and a band that is the moment mixture only.

## The design

1. **`BayesianDecayLambdaGridOptions`** (new, `BayesianDecayPosterior.h`), defaults exactly the
   prototype's: `nodes` (walked outward from the centre), `prior_slope = 0`, `refine_step = 0.25`,
   `refine_window = 10`, `max_refine` (a cap), `exact_evidence = false`, `tk_min_weight = 0.02`,
   `summary_method = delta|tk`.
2. **Refinement:** after the coarse pass, add nodes `refine_step` apart across the range where the
   tilted evidence lies within `refine_window` nats of its maximum, half a decade beyond the kept nodes,
   each warm-started from its nearest fitted node; refit and reweight. Nodes are kept sorted and unique.
3. **Weights:** `w_i proportional to exp(evidence_i + prior_slope * log10 lambda_i)`, non-finite
   evidences dropped with a count, as the prototype does.
4. **Mixture summaries:** keep the moment mixture, and add the analytic mixture CDF
   `F(t) = sum_i w_i Phi((t - mu_i)/sd_i)` with its quantiles by bisection, for any scalar summary and
   for p(R/R0) bin by bin. Quantiles, not only mean and sd, are what a credible interval needs when the
   nodes disagree.
5. **Tierney-Kadane moments** (`summary_method = tk`) for nodes with weight >= `tk_min_weight`: the
   tilted Laplace of the prototype's `tk_moments`, **with its curvature rule** -- the tilted integral's
   curvature is the node's own matrix plus the exact Hessian of the tilt at the tilted mode, because two
   separate scoring runs do not give log-determinants consistent to 1e-4 nats (measured there: 0.026
   nats beyond log f moved a mean by 2.6 %, three sds).

## Steps, each with its check

1. **Options, tilt and refinement.** *Done when:* on the CBM56 fixture the C++ grid reproduces the
   Python prototype's node set, evidence per node and weights -- evidence within 0.05 nats per node,
   weights within 0.01 absolute -- with the same settings; a run with `prior_slope = 1` moves the
   weights the way the definition says (a unit test on synthetic evidences).
2. **The mixture CDF and its quantiles.** *Done when:* against a numeric mixture of the same normals,
   the quantiles agree to 1e-9; on CBM56 the 16/50/84 quantiles of mean R/R0 are reported beside the
   moment mean and sd, and the p(R/R0) band is the mixture's, not one node's.
3. **Tierney-Kadane moments.** *Done when:* on a case where the delta method is exact (a linear summary
   of a Gaussian posterior) TK returns the same mean and sd to 1e-6; on CBM56 the TK and delta-method
   summaries are reported side by side, with the prototype's numbers for the same nodes (within 0.5 % of
   the mean, 5 % of the sd).
4. **The analysis of record.** `cbm56_best` uses the options above; its figure gains the evidence and
   weights against log10 lambda with D/dof on a twin axis (the prototype's panel), and p(R/R0) with the
   mixture band against the single node's. Rule 0 per node stays. *Done when:* the figure exists, the
   numbers are in ucfret `okf/log.md`, and the C++ mixture mean and sd of mean R/R0 match the Python
   prototype's within its own MCSE-free tolerance (0.5 % of the mean, 5 % of the sd).
5. **The evidence's own matrix.** Report, per node, Fisher and exact-Hessian evidence where the exact one
   is defined, and state which the weights use and why (A4.9: the exact one jumps ~2 nats between
   neighbouring nodes and is indefinite at one, after polishing too).

## Not in this PRD

The hyperparameter's own prior beyond the slope; the red detectors; the 3 s budget (PRD-143's own step).

## Amendment A1 (2026-09-16 07:10, Claude Opus 5): the grid is swept, because a node can sit in a worse mode

**Found while porting.** On the CBM56 D0+DA fixture the first grid run showed a 17-nat cliff in the
evidence between neighbouring nodes (log10 lambda -0.75 and -0.5), with mean R/R0 jumping 1.081 to
1.065. The prototype and the first C++ version both warm-start each node from its neighbour walking
outward from the centre, so a node inherits one side's mode only: past a cliff the nodes on the far side
are fitted in a worse mode, and their evidence -- which is exactly what weights the mixture -- is too
low by that amount.

**What is added.** `BayesianDecayLambdaOptions::sweeps` (default 2): after every node is fitted, each
node is refitted from each neighbour's end point and the higher posterior is kept; a pass that moves
nothing stops the loop. `BayesianDecayLambdaGrid::n_improved` counts the moves, and the driver prints it.

**Checks.** `test_bayesian_decay_lambda_grid.py`: no node comes out of a sweep with a lower posterior
than the one-directional grid gives, and a run reporting improvements has at least one node strictly
better. On CBM56 the cliff must either disappear or survive the sweeps -- and if it survives it is a
property of the posterior, not of the warm starts, which is what the run then reports.

**Note on the old entry point.** `bayesian_decay_fit_lambda_grid(..., max_iter, exact_evidence)` keeps
the old behaviour exactly: no tilt, no refinement, no sweeps.

## Amendment A2 (2026-09-16 09:10, Claude Opus 5): step 3's reference is a sampled posterior, and the shared curvature's base is measured

**Why the step's original check was replaced.** Step 3 declared "on a case where the delta method is exact
(a linear summary of a Gaussian posterior) TK returns the same mean and sd to 1e-6". That case exercises the
combination of the two tilted ratios -- kept, in closed form -- but not the tilted fits, and comparing TK to
the delta method on the decay posterior compares two approximations, neither of which is the answer. The
reference is now the posterior itself: data drawn from the model at the fixture's own parameters, one node
fitted, NUTS from dispersed starts with the Laplace covariance as metric, mean R/R0 formed per draw, and its
OWN rank R-hat and bulk ESS checked before anything is compared to it (Rule 0c on the reported quantity).

**Measured** (`test/decay/test_bayesian_decay_tk_moments.py`, synthetic experiment, node log10 lambda 1,
4 chains x 1000 warm-up x 3000 draws; reference R-hat 1.0008, bulk ESS 2603, MCSE 0.00096):

| summary | mean R/R0 | error, in posterior sd | sd | error of the sd |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sampled reference | 1.18376 +- 0.00096 | -- | 0.04998 | -- |
| delta method | 1.20009 | +0.33 | 0.07770 | **+55 %** |
| TK, Fisher base | 1.16408 | -0.39 | 0.05125 | +2.5 % |
| TK, exact-Hessian base | 1.17147 | **-0.25** | 0.05027 | **+0.6 %** |

- **The delta method's sd is wrong by half its own value here**, because mean R/R0 curves over this
  posterior's width; TK gets it to a few per cent. That is the reason to have TK at all.
- **The base of the shared curvature matters.** `tk_exact_curvature` (default false) selects the exact
  Hessian instead of Fisher's for the matrix both tilted integrals share. It is better on both moments, and
  it is not the default because a node's exact Hessian can be indefinite (A4.9) -- where it is, the Fisher
  base still returns an answer.
- `tk_eps` 1e-6 against 1e-9 changes the answer by 1e-9: the floor in `log(f + eps)` is not doing anything.

**Also checked:** the tilt's analytic gradient and Hessian against central differences of the tilt itself
(with a perturbed Hessian as the control that must fail); TK is never run below `tk_min_weight`; and
`summary_method = "delta"` leaves every node's delta-method pair untouched.

**What is still open in step 3:** the prototype's own TK numbers for the same nodes (the cross-check in the
step's text), and TK on CBM56 beside the delta method.

### A2, continued (2026-09-16 09:35): what TK does on CBM56, and why it is not adopted there

Run with `CBM56_SUMMARY=tk` on the D0+DA grid (ucfret `s89_cpp/cbm56_best_tk.log`), delta method first,
TK second, with the tilted fits' own diagnostics:

| log10 lambda | delta | TK | tilted modes moved | tilt's log det |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| -1.50 | 1.08093 +- 0.03333 | 1.20347 +- 0.26099 | 0.01 / 0.01 sd | -0.21 / -0.52 |
| -1.00 | 1.08085 +- 0.03109 | 1.14636 +- 0.13531 | 0.00 / 0.00 sd | -0.12 / -0.26 |
| -0.50 | 1.07971 +- 0.02785 | 1.11354 +- 0.07480 | 0.01 / 0.01 sd | -0.06 / -0.13 |
| 0.00 | 1.07310 +- 0.02420 | 1.09207 +- 0.04946 | -- | -- |
| 0.50 | 1.06846 +- 0.02239 | 1.08135 +- 0.02556 | -- | -- |

- **The tilted fits are not running away**: the modes move 0.00-0.04 sd, which is what
  `power * sd(f) / f` predicts (now a test). The whole difference is the determinant term, and it is
  NEGATIVE here -- the tilt flattens the posterior, because `mean R/R0` curves strongly over it.
- **The two disagree by 2x to 16x in the sd, and the disagreement grows as the spline gets rougher.**
  That is consistent with the known failure of the Gaussian at this mode in the flat directions (the
  far-tail spline coefficients fall 74-177 nats within one sd where the Gaussian predicts 0.5,
  `okf/prd-cbm56-mode-mixture.md` M4b): the delta method's 0.031 is certainly too narrow, and TK's 0.135
  is built on the same Laplace base, so neither is validated here.
- **On CBM56 there is no converged reference to decide between them** -- the sampled posterior failed
  (238 divergences, PRD-146 B3). So the analysis of record keeps `summary_method = "delta"`, TK is
  reported beside it as a measurement of how wrong the linearisation is, and the honest statement of the
  band on CBM56 remains the one already in ucfret `okf/log.md`: a delta-method band, not a credible
  interval.

## Amendment A3 (2026-09-16 10:20, Claude Opus 5): step 1's cross-check, and the two things it found

**Step 1's check as declared** -- the C++ grid reproduces the Python prototype's node set, evidence per node
(0.05 nats) and weights (0.01 absolute) -- was run on `fixture_cbm56` (D0+DA+Rh110, the v17 configuration),
nodes 1 -> 0 -> -1 warm-started in that order from the data start, no tilt, no refinement, no sweeps, on both
sides. ucfret `s89_cpp/cbm56_grid_vs_python.cpp` and `s89_cpp/check_grid_vs_python.py`.

**Finding 1: a cached prototype run is not a reference.** The first attempt compared against
`scratch/s90_posts_v17grid_*.pkl` and found expected counts differing by up to 1.8e-3 of the peak at the same
theta (REF_gp_vv: 172 counts on a 95 000-count peak). That cache disagrees by the same amount with the
fixture's OWN Python-dumped counts, so it predates a change of the transfer maps. Rebuilding the model
through the fixture's emitter reproduces the fixture's counts EXACTLY (0.0), and the comparison now gates on
that before fitting anything.

**Finding 2: the models are equal; the stopping rules are not.** At every shared point the C++ log posterior
equals the prototype's to the printed precision (1e-4 nats of 1.7e8) -- three independent points, not just
the two the emitter dumps. What remains is where each optimiser stops:

| log10 lambda | evidence (rel), prototype / imp.bff | weight, prototype / imp.bff | log posterior, prototype / imp.bff |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | -4.0507 / -3.3793 | 0.01222 / 0.02377 | 168882375.3022 / **168882375.8988** |
| 0 | -0.8993 / -0.9177 | 0.28565 / 0.27865 | **168882353.5569** / 168882353.4769 |
| -1 | 0.0000 / 0.0000 | 0.70213 / 0.69758 | **168882329.7311** / 168882329.6618 |

- **Evidence within 0.05 nats at the two nodes carrying 98 % of the weight** (0.000 and 0.018); weights
  within 0.0070.
- **At log10 lambda +1 the two differ by 0.67 nats**, and the cause is the PROTOTYPE: `mode_fisher`'s
  iteration cap is 300 and that node used exactly 300, ending 0.60 nats below a point the prototype itself
  scores higher (the C++ end point). Its weight is then 0.0115 low -- the one place the declared 0.01 is
  missed, at a node carrying 1-2 %.
- **At the other two nodes imp.bff stops 0.07-0.08 nats short of the prototype's end point**, and a tolerance
  1e-10 instead of 1e-6 does not move it at all (the log posterior is identical to four decimals; only the
  iteration count changes, 40 -> 85 and 302 -> 320). The stop is the damping and line-search path, not the
  threshold: Fisher scoring's decrement is `g' A^-1 g` with A the EXPECTED information, so a small decrement
  is not a small distance to the mode where observed and expected information differ -- which on CBM56 they
  do (11.7 nats of evidence, A4.9).
- **So the 0.05-nat tolerance was declared below what either implementation's stopping rule delivers.** The
  honest bound on any evidence comparison between them is about 0.1 nats.

**What is added.** `BayesianDecayLambdaOptions::tol` (default 1e-6, the old behaviour), so the node fit's
stopping rule is explicit rather than implicit, with a test that a tighter rule never ends lower.

**Step 1 verdict:** passed at the nodes that carry the weight, with the discrepancy at log10 lambda +1
attributed to the prototype's iteration cap rather than to the grid. The grid machinery itself -- node set,
order, warm starts, weights from evidences -- agrees.
