## Why

A shipped rotamer library is *one fixed sample of the free dye*, reweighted
at a site and never resampled; at a tight site the effective sample size falls
to 1.7 conformers and moves E by 0.07
([`site_dependent_rotamer_sampling.md`](../validation/site_dependent_rotamer_sampling.md)).
That page's own conclusion — "the real fix is resampling, not reweighting" —
is what a site-conditioned generative model does. Three earlier attempts at a
learned dye model never learned from MD: PRD-110 stopped on identifiability,
PRD-135 learns the PDE solver, PRD-119 plans an MSM on the *free* dye. The
question the user brought back from a conference — Timewarp — is the missing
one: learn `p(x(t+Δt) | x(t))` from MD pairs at a large lag, transferably.

**Prior art (2026-09-07).** Nobody has published a learned, site-conditioned,
time-coarsened model of dye+linker dynamics. Closest: hand-built Langevin dye
models (Frost, Cook & Sanabria, *Phys Rev Res* 2025; spherical dyes, no
sticking, no site transfer), FRETpredict's free-dye libraries (2024), and the
2026 *JPCB* review (Sauve et al.) which lists no neural dye surrogate. The
machinery exists: Timewarp (Klein et al., NeurIPS 2023; flow proposal + MH,
2–4 aa peptides, no speedup beyond), ITO (NeurIPS 2023) and **TITO**
(Viguera Diez, Schreiner, Olsson, *Sci Adv* 2026, arXiv 2510.07589): PaiNN
velocity field, OT flow matching, conditioned on `x(t)` and the lag, trained
jointly on 12,530 small molecules (MDQM9-nc) and 1,457 tetrapeptides, no
energy at inference, Boltzmann marginals (JSD ≤ 0.35) and implied timescales
over three decades on unseen molecules, four orders of magnitude faster than
MD. UniSim (ICML 2025), FBM, MDGen, BioEmu, CGSchNet are the neighbours. The
user's own `avnn` (AV grid → 3D MAE → observable heads) did not work and is a
negative result.

## Decisions (tpeulen, 2026-09-07, by interview)

1. **State**: Cartesian, SE(3)-equivariant, TITO-faithful — not torsion space.
2. **Model**: `p(x_{t+Δt} | x_t, Δt, env)`, multi-lag training over
   {5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500 ps, 1, 2, 5 ns}; the environment is the
   protein heavy atoms within ~25 Å of the anchor as a point cloud with
   {element, residue class from `qpinn/voxel.py`, backbone/side-chain,
   distance to anchor}; free dye = empty environment, one model for both arms.
3. **No Metropolis correction in the product** (TITO's choice; no energy
   consistent with explicit-water training exists). Energies under the dry
   prmtop + GB serve as a validity diagnostic only.

## The verdict, 2026-09-14

Answering the owner's question -- *does TITO work for dyes* -- against the bars set on 09-07. **Partly, and the half
that fails is diagnosed rather than mysterious.**

**Free dye (Alexa 488 maleimide), `free_v4`, 400 epochs on 12.9 µs of MD over 65 runs.**

| criterion | bar | result | |
|---|---|---|---|
| G2 rotation, max \|ΔC(t)\| | ≤ 0.10 | **0.090** (held-out dye 0.098) | **pass** |
| G3 validity, invalid frames | < 1 % | **0.6 %** after chirality repair | **pass**, with a caveat below |
| G1 torsion marginals, JSD | ≤ 0.067 (ensemble floor), 0.050 (at the model's sample size) | 0.128 | fail, ~1.9× the floor |
| PRD-116 library parity, overlap | ≥ 0.815 | 0.696 | fail |
| speed | ≥ 100× MD | 13× at a 100 ps lag, ~125× at 1 ns | pass at the lag that matters |

The two failures are one defect: **the model builds the dye too compact.** Mean anchor-to-dye reach is 9.1 ± 4.0 Å
against 12.1 ± 5.0 Å in MD, so the linker is under-extended, which moves the torsion marginals and the library overlap
together. Chirality is the other known defect: the network is O(3)-equivariant and cannot tell a molecule from its
mirror image, the pseudo-scalar feature did not teach it, and the repair (reflect enantiomers, drop diastereomers)
throws away **36 %** of the raw output. So the honest claim is that the rotational dynamics -- the observable this
project exists for -- are reproduced, while the conformational distribution is not yet.

**Site MD (arm B).** 149 of 176 runs, 3.7 µs, 29 measured maleimide sites on T4L, hGBP1 and MalE apo/holo, six 25 ns
restarts each. Two results, and the second is the important one:

- The dye is surface-bound at every site (ring within 4 Å of the protein in 89-100 % of frames), with rotational
  correlation 1.8-4.7 ns against 0.135 ns free.
- **A site's dye distribution is not converged at this sampling, and the restarts prove it.** Two halves of one 25 ns
  run overlap at 0.45 (median over 148 runs); two different restarts of the *same site* overlap at **0.01-0.15**. Six
  restarts are six nearly disjoint samples, not one ensemble. Consequently the predicted residual anisotropy has the
  right population mean (0.150 ± 0.057 against 0.140 ± 0.039 measured, bias +0.010 over 24 sites) and **no site-to-site
  discrimination whatever** (Pearson −0.09, Spearman +0.03): the per-seed scatter, ±0.09 in r_inf, is twice the spread
  between sites.

**Site-conditioned model, first held-out numbers (`cond_v4`, 2026-09-14, T4L 132 -- a site it never trained on).**
Three restarts scored: dye-density overlap with the MD **0.16, 0.47, 0.38** (the within-run half-vs-half control is
0.45, so the best of the three is as close to the MD as the MD is to itself); contact fraction 0.88-0.93 against the
MD's, within 0.07-0.12; chirality repair keeps 92-96 %, far better than the free dye's 64 %. And **rotation fails
outright: max |dC(t)| 0.72-0.95 against a 0.1 bar**, where the free-dye model reaches 0.09. So the conditioned model
learns *where* the dye sits at a site but not *how it turns* -- the exact mirror image of the free-dye model's
result, and a pointer at the anchored frame and the fixed environment nodes rather than at the data.

**What that means for the method.** The site-conditioned model cannot be validated against this reference, because the
reference is not converged -- a gate with a floor of 0.05 is a gate on nothing. It also means a surrogate is the only
affordable route to the observable: converging one site needs far more than the 150 ns MD gives, and 1 µs per site is
1450 GPU-h by MD against 60-120 GPU-h through the model.

**Verdict: the free-dye half is real (rotation, validity, speed), the conformational half needs work (compactness,
chirality), and the site half is blocked on reference sampling rather than on the network.** For the conference this is
a result in three parts: a published transferable MD surrogate silently drops rotational dynamics and chirality until
it is made to keep them; a dye at a labelling site is unconverged in 150 ns of restarts; and the MD reproduces the
average anisotropy but not the per-site ranking.

  steps per lag step, i.e. **0.3 µs of dye time per GPU-hour at a 100 ps lag and ~2.5 µs/GPU-h at a 1 ns lag** (13x and
  125x the 20 ns/GPU-h the fragment MD gets on the same card). The site-conditioned model carries 500-1500 environment
  atoms and should be 5-10x slower, so ~1 µs per site in 2-4 GPU-h. Consequence for the campaign: **1 µs per site by MD
  is out of reach** (29 maleimide sites x 1 µs = 1450 GPU-h against ~250-300 available before the conference), while the
  same target is 60-120 GPU-h through the surrogate -- which is the argument for building it, stated in the units the
  audience cares about.
- **Throughput of the campaign, measured (2026-09-08).** Three MD lanes share GPU 0 through per-job locks. On MalE-sized
  fragments (22.5k atoms) they gave 484 ns/day while the conditional model trained on the same card, and **1018 ns/day**
  once that training was paused -- the trainer was costing more than half the MD. The site model is therefore paused
  until 60 site runs exist (`s09_loop.sh MIN_RUNS=60`), which also means it is retrained on 29 sites instead of 13.
  Queue: 176 jobs x 25 ns = 4.4 µs, ETA ~2026-09-12; that is 150 ns per site over all 29 maleimide sites, extensible to
  ~300 ns per site by the conference.
- **The campaign is now restarts, not long runs (2026-09-08).** The dye sticks for 0.2-25 ns at a time, so what limits a
  site's ensemble is how many independent binding modes are seen, not the length of one trajectory. `s07_campaign.sh`
  runs 6 restarts x 25 ns per site from six different library rotamers, over **all 29 measured maleimide sites** --
  T4L (5), hGBP1 (8), MalE apo 1OMP (8) and MalE holo 1ANF (8) -- 176 jobs, ~150 ns per site, ~220 GPU-h. PSD-95 is not
  in it: the measurement loader excludes it (AlphaFold model, construct not represented). The ten T4L pAcF sites need
  the `B1R` linker and their seeds are placed, but they are queued behind the maleimide set.

- **In fragment MD the dye is surface-bound almost all the time (2026-09-08, 13 sites x 25 ns, first seed).** The
  Alexa 488 ring is within 4 Å of the protein in **89-100 %** of frames, with contact dwell times of 0.2-25 ns, and the
  transition dipole keeps correlation for **1.8-4.7 ns** against 0.135 ns for the free dye. Turned into an observable,
  `r_inf = r0 x C(5 ns)` gives **0.128 ± 0.089 against 0.136 ± 0.058 measured** on the four usable hGBP1 sites -- the
  right magnitude, no bias (-0.008), but the site-to-site *ranking* does not follow (rank correlation -0.60 at n = 4,
  one seed each; `1F5N_A496` is excluded, its run was still short when scored). So the MD reproduces how ordered the
  dye is on average but not yet which site is more ordered than which, which is what the second seeds and the whole-
  protein runs are for. AMBER-DYES is known to over-stack dyes, so this is also a force-field statement, not only a
  sampling one. Trajectories for inspection: `/Volumes/SD1TB/imp-bff-dye-md/dtw/site_inspect/` (protein-superposed,
  with `cloud_*.png` overlays).

- **The model's weak bond is the thiosuccinimide C–S, and a PDB hides it while a viewer exaggerates it (2026-09-08).**
  Visual inspection of the exported trajectories showed the molecule in floating pieces. The coordinates are bonded --
  the caps sit 1.34 Å from the linker in every frame -- but PyMOL ignores CONECT by default, bonds by distance, and
  drops any heavy-atom pair beyond ~1.9 Å, so one stretched bond detaches everything downstream. The stretched bond is
  real and specific: `C1R.C6–S1` (the maleimide–cysteine link) averages 1.77 Å in the model against 1.83 Å in MD and
  exceeds 1.9 Å in **7 % of model frames against 2 % of MD frames**. Consequences: the export now writes a `.mol2` with
  an explicit bond block beside the PDB (`s05d_export_traj.py`), and G3's "broken bond" threshold must be tied to the
  MD distribution per bond, not to a flat 2.5 Å, which had reported 0 %.
  What the visual inspection did establish, measured on the same samples: the dye is chemically recognisable and its
  **aromatic rings are as planar as MD's** (out-of-plane rms 0.031 Å against 0.032 Å) with ring C–C 1.387 ± 0.035 Å
  against 1.404 ± 0.027 Å; the defect is confined to the two junction bonds (thiosuccinimide C–S and the dye–linker
  N99–C99), which exceed 1.9 Å in 5.9 % of frames and split the molecule there for a distance-bonding viewer. The dye's
  internal heavy-atom RMSD to a fixed MD geometry is 1.48 ± 0.50 Å against MD's own 0.88 ± 0.21 Å.

- **PaiNN cannot tell a molecule from its mirror image, and TITO inherits it (2026-09-08).** With the orientation fix in
  place the rotation is right, but the two real stereocentres of the C1R linker (CA and C7) are inverted in ~71 % of
  sampled frames, i.e. at random. The cause is architectural: PaiNN's features are vectors combined by scalar products,
  so the network is equivariant under the full O(3) including reflections and carries no reflection-odd quantity. Fix:
  `dtw/model.py::pseudo_scalar` feeds one reflection-odd number per node (the normalised triple product of its three
  lowest-index neighbours, for the conditioning frame and the interpolated one) through a zero-initialised linear layer,
  so a warm start is exact and training decides how much handedness to use; `free_v4` fine-tunes from `free_v3`.
- **Two of my own measurements were wrong, and both flattered the failure (2026-09-08).** (i) The chirality check took
  every heavy atom with four heavy neighbours, which on Alexa 488 is the two *sulfonate sulfurs* — three equivalent
  oxygens, so the signed volume flips when they are permuted — while missing the real stereocentres, which carry a
  hydrogen. It now uses RDKit's stereocentres on the reference geometry. (ii) The torsion-marginal floor came from
  28,000-frame MD ensembles while the model histogram held 800 frames; the finite-sample bias alone is a large part of
  the gap (MD scores 0.050 against itself at 3,200 frames). The gate now computes the floor from two disjoint MD
  subsamples *of the model's own sample size* and prints it beside the ensemble floor; probes run 64 chains × 100 steps.
- **Chirality is not the whole marginal gap.** Removing handedness from both sides (symmetrised histograms) moves the
  A48_C1R JSD only 0.205 → 0.177, against a 0.067 ensemble floor, so there is a real conformational gap as well as the
  chirality defect — how much of it is finite-sample is what the corrected floor will say.

- **TITO as published does not model rotation or preserve chirality (2026-09-08, `free_v2` at 71k steps, A48_C1R).**
  Its velocity field discards the conditioning frame's equivariant features and substitutes random vectors, so the
  network knows the conformation of x(t) but not its orientation or handedness; the sampled orientation follows the
  noise. Measured: the model's dipole autocorrelation is 0.00 at the first lag (MD 0.44), 36–43 % of frames are complete
  mirror images and 32 % mixed, torsion JSD 0.22 against a 0.067 floor. Harmless for a free-molecule conformational
  sampler, disqualifying for anisotropy. Fix: `dtw/model.py::OrientedVelocity` keeps the conditioning vector features
  (anchored variant for arm B), trained with permutation-only OT; `free_v3` fine-tunes from `free_v2`. Also measured:
  100 Euler steps instead of 20 cut broken bonds 48 → 6.5 % and clashes 6.5 → 1.8 %; the probes now use 100.

- **Gold set chemistry (2026-09-07).** Of the 15 measured Alexa488 T4L sites, ten (5, 8, 19, 22, 36, 44, 55, 60, 69, 70)
  carry the dye through a p-acetyl-phenylalanine hydroxylamine linkage — the ones with `r_inf` — and only five (86, 119,
  127, 132, 150; lifetimes and QY only) are cysteine-maleimide. All eight hGBP1 sites are cysteine-maleimide with `r_inf`.
  The C1R campaign's gold set is therefore 13 sites (`data/sites_gold.csv`), and the experimental `r_inf` check rests on
  hGBP1; the pAcF sites need their own linker parameters (a `dye_library` job) before they can be simulated.
- **Builder (`s06_build_site.py`).** A site's start conformer comes from FRETpredict's own site logic as shipped in
  `IMP.bff` (`RotamerEnsemble.from_site`, the cutoff10 library placed on the backbone frame and Boltzmann-screened —
  user's call, 2026-09-07, `s06a_place_rotamers.py`): replica 0 the top-weight rotamer, later replicas weight-
  proportional draws; the ensemble's Z and effective sample size are recorded per site (T4L 127: ESS 5.7, T4L 86: 115).
  A Kabsch graft of a free-dye frame remains the fallback when no seed exists; carving a 20 Å fragment
  with ACE/NME caps (the NME methyl is `C` in this AmberTools, not `CH3`), tleap with the N99–C99 bond and detected
  disulfides, OpenMM on OpenCL (heinzehub's driver cannot JIT the conda CUDA PTX) with 2 kcal/mol/Å² backbone restraints
  on fragments; T4L site 44 as a fragment is 60 residues / 14,161 atoms with water.

## Risks

- **Site generalisation** — the failure mode of `avnn` and of the early PINN
  (0.029 ns trained vs 0.257 ns held-out structures). Local equivariant
  conditioning, many short runs over many sites, and G8 as the go/no-go.
- **Force field, not nature** — AMBER-DYES/ff14SB sticking; only stage 6
  against measured `r_inf`/`ρ` says anything about reality.
- **Cross-dye transfer is thin at 17 labels** (TITO used 12,530 molecules);
  per-dye models are the fallback, Alexa488 and Alexa647 are what is paid for.
- **Compute** — heinzehub's two GPUs are shared with the PRD-116 dye-library lanes; cordeshub is saturated by the
  PRD-135 trainer. Allocation (owner, 2026-09-07, arranged with rotamer-simulation, revised the same evening): the
  dye-library work runs a 24-job closing queue (~4.2 days on both cards; `A48_B1R` × 3 second in it), after which
  **both cards go to this campaign** (`s07` starts on its own when no `make_library.py` holds GPU 0; a second lane
  on GPU 1 then). `free_v1` training shares GPU 1 meanwhile (~14 % cost to their lane, accepted). When the campaign
  ends, `s07` writes `GPU0_RELEASED` — the owner's rule that the cards return to the rotamer simulations. Stages 0–3
  deliver the library generator without any new MD.
