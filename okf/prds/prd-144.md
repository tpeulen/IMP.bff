# PRD-144: a fitted response tail per detector for the Bayesian decay model

**Owner** (tpeulen, 2026-09-15, ucfret prompts 453-455): "sure that this is not an l1/l2
issue, in RH110?" -- "irf?" -- "2" (option 2: a response-tail model fitted jointly).
**Author:** Claude Opus 5 for tpeulen.

## Why

On CBM56 the Rh110 perpendicular histogram (REF_gs_vh) fails Rule 0: D/dof 1.352, z +4.40,
with a ±1 % wave over 0-17 ns (ucfret `okf/log.md`, 2026-09-15). What has been excluded:
- l1 and l2 (free: 7 % of the excess);
- a Rh110 timing offset;
- the IRF's counting noise (six resampled IRFs).

What remains:
- The wave depends on which water IRF is used (h20_2 instead of h20: runs p 1.6e-4 -> 0.34,
  but the excess stays).
- It replicates across two Rh110 measurements.
- A data/fit profile transfers between them.

So it is either a detector nonlinearity or a **response mismatch**: the water-scatter IRF is
recorded at the laser wavelength, the dye's photons are green, and a single-photon detector's
response has a wavelength-dependent diffusion tail. This PRD adds the second as a model element
that the data can fit or reject.

## The model

For a physical detector `d` the prepared response `u` (background removed, shifted, clamped,
unit sum) stays the SCATTER column: scattered light is at the laser wavelength. The response
to FLUORESCENCE is

    u'_d = (1 - a_d) u + a_d (u (*) k(tau_d))

where `k(tau)` is the exact bin-integrated periodic exponential kernel of unit sum
(`periodic_decay_kernel`, order 0). Consequences:
- `u'` keeps unit sum, so the amplitudes stay counts.
- At `a_d = 0` the model is today's, exactly.
- Parameters per physical detector, shared by every sample: `a_d` (logit on [0, 0.3], uniform)
  and `log10 tau_d` (logit on [log10 0.02, log10 5] ns, uniform).
- The lifetime columns are `u' (*) K_c`. Since
  `u' (*) K_c = (1 - a) u (*) K_c + a u (*) k (*) K_c`, the kernel FFT products do this without
  a second pass.
- Derivatives:
  - `d/da = u (*) (k - delta) (*) K_c`, renormalised as the columns are;
  - `d k / d ln tau = k1 - k0` exactly for the bin-integrated periodic kernels, since
    `d/dtau [e^{-t/tau}/tau] = (t e^{-t/tau}/tau^2 - e^{-t/tau}/tau)/tau`. Order 1 exists in
    tttrlib.

Limits:
- A tail makes the fluorescence response broader, never narrower.
- One exponential is the simplest tail; a second is out of scope until one is shown to be
  insufficient.

## Steps, each with its check

1. **`bayesian_response_basis` gains the tail** (`BayesianMeasuredResponse.h`): `tail_fraction`,
   `tail_log10_tau` inputs, the scatter column untouched, and tangents `dB_tail_fraction` and
   `dB_tail_log10_tau`.
   - *Done when* the tangents equal central differences (best of steps, as the shift and
     background tangents are checked), `a = 0` reproduces today's basis bit for bit, columns keep
     unit sum, and a planted error in the tau tangent is caught.
   - Test: `test_bayesian_measured_response.py` extended.
2. **The decay model reads it** (`BayesianDecayModel.h`): the responses table gains optional
   `tail_fraction_var` and `tail_log10_tau_var`; the count Jacobian gains their columns.
   - *Done when* the synthetic experiment with a tail passes the count-Jacobian check on every
     column and ucfret's CBM56 gates (no tail) are unchanged.
3. **CBM56 variants** (ucfret `s89_cpp`): tails on gp and gs, fitted from the polished mode with
   declared starts, each judged by Rule 0 per histogram with a residual figure:
   - the record (rhd110 with h20);
   - the replicate (rhd110_2 with h20_2);
   - rhd110 with h20_2.
   - Physical tail parameters must agree between the replicates within their sd.
   - *Done when* the table and figure are logged, whether REF_gs_vh passes or not:
     - if it passes with consistent parameters, the response mismatch is the explanation;
     - if it does not, the tail model is rejected and the nonlinearity remains.
   - Negative control: a tail on gp only must not fix gs.
