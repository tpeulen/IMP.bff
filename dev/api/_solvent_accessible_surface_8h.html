IMPBFFEXPORT std::vector< double > fibonacci_sphere_points ( int n)

n points on the unit sphere, by the Fibonacci lattice.

Point sits at polar angle and azimuth , so the points are equal-area and none sits on a pole. Returned flat, three doubles per point, as (x, y, z) with z along the polar axis.

A second golden spiral beside sphere_points(), and deliberately so: this is the point set probe_solvent_exposure() samples, and the quenching analysis it was ported from (Shrake-Rupley water exposure of a dye at every accessible-volume voxel) pinned its exposures against this one. The two sets differ by a rotation, so a covered/uncovered count is not the same for both.

Solvent exposure of a spherical probe at each of many centres.

The Shrake-Rupley test for a probe that is not one of the atoms: the probe (a dye, radius probe_radius) sits at a centre, a water molecule (radius water_radius) touches it where the water's centre lies on the sphere of radius around that centre, and a sample point there is covered when it is closer than to some atom (strict). The exposure is the uncovered fraction of the n_points samples of fibonacci_sphere_points(): 1 in open water, 0 buried.

The probe itself, its linker and any other probe do not cover anything; only the listed atoms do. Neighbours come from a cell list, so the cost is linear in the number of centres.

Solvent-accessible surface area of selected atoms (Shrake-Rupley).

Samples each selected atom's surface at radius from its centre and counts the samples no other atom occludes within .

The neighbour cutoff is **, which is the tight criterion for this kernel: a sample sits radius from its own centre and is occluded by a neighbour within of it. The kernel this was ported from tested a squared distance against an unsquared sum, and against the van der Waals radius rather than the sampling radius – a 2.45 A cutoff where 6.0 A was meant, so nearly every occluding neighbour was missed and the area came back far too large. Fixed in PRD-109; nothing downstream moved, because nothing consumed the result.

Solvent-accessible surface area with a per-atom radius (Shrake-Rupley).

The textbook form, and the one a relative accessibility needs: atom i is sampled on a sphere of radius and a sample is occluded when it falls within of another atom's centre, so each atom is occluded by its neighbour's real size. The area returned is the accessible surface .

solvent_accessible_surface_area() above differs deliberately and is not a worse version of this: it samples every atom at one radius and scales the count by , which is what the contact and quenching estimates want (a probe of fixed size rolling over a uniform obstacle set) and is cheaper. The two answer different questions; a caller comparing an area against a tabulated maximum wants this one.
