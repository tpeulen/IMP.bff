Asymmetric chi-squared contribution of one distance restraint.

. The residual is model minus data, which is the definition the whole package uses: a model distance that is too large is a positive deviation. is the experimental error on that side – a positive residual is judged against error_pos, a negative one against error_neg. Squaring makes the sign invisible in the value; what the convention decides is which error bar applies, and with asymmetric errors that is the whole answer.

A non-positive error contributes nothing rather than dividing by zero.

The same with FPS's force cap: harmonic near zero, linear far.

FPS's SpringEngine.ForceAndTorque (SpringEngine.cs:432-443) does not score a plain parabola. With and (SpringEngine.cs:141-142) it scores

– a Huber-like robustification, joined : past the knee the force is exactly max_force and the cost grows only linearly, so one grossly violated restraint cannot dominate a docking run. A pure harmonic does not reproduce FPS under docking's MaxForce = 400, which is the situation every random restart starts in.

max_force is stated in FPS's units – it caps of the this returns, so the knee sits at exactly where FPS puts it. A restraint that scores half a (which is what a Gaussian is, and what #IMP::bff::AVPairDistanceMeasurement::score_model returns) therefore feels half that slope; the knee position, which is what changes a docked answer, is unaffected by the factor.

The cap engages on tight restraints and leaves loose ones alone: with Å and max_force = 400 the knee is at 2 Å, with Å it is at 1800 Å and the cap is inert.

Parameters
[in]max_forcethe force cap; non-positive means no cap, and #IMP::bff::chi2_score is then returned unchanged
See also
IMP::bff::chi2_score for the sign convention (model minus data, and which error bar that selects)

Apply a named transfer function to a mean-position distance.

The dispatch an fps.json calibration names, in one place. "None" and the Rmp convention return rmp untouched; "Gaussian" applies gaussian_rmp_to_rda_mean(); "Polynomial" evaluates coeffs through polynomial_transfer_ascending() and falls back to the Gaussian when no coefficients were given – a calibration that names a polynomial and carries none is a calibration that has not been fitted yet, and sigma_rda is the parametric stand-in for it.

[in]coeffspolynomial coefficients, lowest power first

Fit (or ) by translation.

The two clouds are rigidly separated along the line joining their mean positions and the target distance is recomputed at seven offsets, then a polynomial of degree is fitted through them. That is what a docking run needs: a cheap during sampling, corrected afterwards.

An empty cloud, a zero total weight, or coincident mean positions give the identity polynomial — there is nothing to correct.

sigma is the per-component width of the separation vector**, not of one cloud and not of the distance distribution. Writing the separation as with , the first-order term averages away and the two transverse components give , so the correction is — not half of it, which is what this returned under one of its two former names until 2026-08-18.

Smallest distance between any point of one cloud and any of the other.

A bound, where every other distance type here is an average: two labels can approach no closer than this, whatever the linkers do. That is the quantity a crosslink-style upper limit is written against, and the reason it is not a mean.

Points of zero weight are skipped – a zero-weight point is not in the volume. The double loop is exact and ; at the few thousand points an AV carries that is milliseconds, and there is no sampled version because a minimum estimated from a sample is biased high.

Parameters
[in]p1,p2flat clouds, four per point: x, y, z, weight
Returns
the minimum distance; infinity when either cloud has no weight

Random distance and weight-product samples between two clouds.

Draws one point from each cloud per sample. The two clouds are independent, so any pairing samples the joint distribution.

The clouds arrive straight from numpy, one (n, 4) array per cloud via IN_ARRAY2. The result is published as an (n_samples, 2) managed numpy view: column 0 is the distance, column 1 the weight product. Both shapes are part of the contract, so a caller never reshapes a flat buffer to recover the picture the data already has.

Parameters
[in]p1,p2flat clouds, four per point. Via IN_ARRAY2, carrying the (n, 4) shape; the values are read as 4 * n in row order.
[in]n_samplessamples to draw
[in]seedfor reproducibility
[out]output,n_output1,n_output2(n_samples, 2) view. The buffer is malloc'd and numpy adopts it.

The inverse of #IMP::bff::cloud_model_distance in its first argument: slide the two clouds apart, rigidly and along the line joining their mean positions, until the modelled observable equals target_distance, and report the at which that happens.

This is the number that turns a measurement into a restraint an ordinary two-particle potential can carry. An experiment reports ; a distance restraint between two mean positions needs ; the two differ by several angstrom and the difference depends on the shapes of both volumes, so it cannot be a constant. #IMP::bff::effective_distance is the forward approximation of the same relation, parametrised rather than measured; this is the exact answer for the two volumes in hand.

The pairs are sampled once and then translated, so the function being bisected is smooth: resampling per iteration would add noise of order to a root that is being located to accuracy. Bracket by expansion, then bisect – which is stricter than the fixed-point iteration of the reference implementation, whose step assumes and which stops when it stops improving rather than when it has converged.

Distances between two labels, whatever represents them.

(formerly IMP/bff/StatesDistance.h, now a section of this file)

Everything here takes #IMP::bff::States, so one implementation serves an accessible volume, a rotamer library, a Gaussian, a coarse-grained ensemble and an MD trajectory. That is the point: this was the third of six implementations of probe-pair distances in the package, and they were checked against each other before being merged — on T4L A132 x A65, 2180 x 3087 points, 2e5 samples, came out 51.945 against 51.940 A and 51.696 against 51.692 A, with identical. The spread is Monte-Carlo sampling noise.

There is one sampler, #IMP::bff::random_distances, so two callers asking for the same quantity compute the same number rather than two estimates of one integral agreeing to .
