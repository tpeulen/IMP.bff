Detailed Description

A dye placed on a structure and moved, as an ordinary simulation object.

Construction places the dye and builds the system; after that it is #IMP::bff::ProbeSimulation like any other – minimize(), step(), run(), positions in and out.

The structure and the dye arrive as #IMP::bff::ProteinFrame, which is arrays: coordinates, atom names, residue names and indices, chain ids. Where they came from is the caller's business – a PDB read by #IMP::bff::load_structure, a frame of a trajectory, a generator. The dye's chemistry – its bonds, angles and torsions – comes from its MOL2, because that is a chemistry file and there is nothing to gain by re-deriving it from coordinates.

Everything IMP is kept behind the pointer: this header names none of it, and neither does anything a wrapper sees.

Place dye on residue of chain in protein and set up.

Parameters
[in]proteinthe structure to label
[in]dyethe dye's atoms, as they sit before placement
[in]dye_mol2the dye's MOL2: its bonds, angles and torsions
[in]chain,residuethe labelled site
[in]parametersa JSON object; empty means the defaults. integrator (md or bd), temperature (K), timestep_fs (negative lets the integrator choose), friction_ps (md only), interaction_sphere (A, how far around the site the protein pushes back), repulsion_k, seed (negative leaves the generator alone), strip_site_sidechain.
Exceptions
ValueExceptionwhen the site is not in the structure, or for an integrator that is neither md nor bd
IOExceptionwhen the MOL2 cannot be read
