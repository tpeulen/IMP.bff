Reduce many species to few, preserving the first two moments exactly.

An accessible volume gives one species per point – tens of thousands – and almost none of them are distinguishable. Species are binned on a uniform grid in the rate constant; each bin returns the summed amplitude and the amplitude-weighted mean rate** of its members.

That choice makes and exact, so the total population and the initial slope are preserved whatever n_bins is. Higher moments are not: the curvature is under-stated by an amount that falls as the square of the bin width, so the reduction is a genuine approximation and the caller chooses how much of one.

Parameters
[in]amplitudesper species
[in]rate_constantsper species, 1/ns
[in]n_binsbins to distribute the rate range over
[out]out_ratesthe amplitude-weighted mean rate of each non-empty bin
Returns
the summed amplitude of each non-empty bin; empty bins are dropped, so the result may be shorter than n_bins

The species average, not the intensity average. Efficiency is defined per molecule – the fraction of excitations that transfer – and the intensity average weights each molecule by how many photons it emitted, which is exactly the thing transfer suppresses. Using it gives an efficiency that is systematically too low, by tens of percent on a heterogeneous sample.

Parameters

[in]donor_acceptorthe same donor with one

The static limit taken literally: if a population of weight decays at and keeps that rate, then is the decay, with no fitting and no approximation. Whether that premise holds is the caller's to know, and exact records the answer.

The spectrum of a state ensemble under a set of interaction terms.

One species per state – per accessible-volume point, per rotamer, per conformer – carrying the summed rate of every channel acting on it. This is the reduction that connects a representation and the photophysics to an experiment-neutral answer, and it is representation-agnostic for the same reason the terms are: it consumes states.

Warning
This is the static limit. It is exact when each state holds its rate for the whole excited-state lifetime, and wrong when the dye reorganises fast enough to average over rates – then the decay is not a sum of exponentials at all and the population has to be propagated instead (#IMP::bff::GridDiffusionSolver, or the Brownian walk). Pass exact = false when using it outside that limit, so the spectrum says what it is.
Parameters
[in]termsthe interaction terms to sum
[in]firstthe dye whose states are being rated; its weights are the populations unless weights overrides them
[in]secondthe second participant, for the 2-body terms
[in]weightspopulation per state; the first participant's own weights when empty, which is what an accessible volume's occupancy already is
[in]exactsee PhotophysicsLifetimeSpectrum

Weighted histogram of the outer product of two distributions.

The distribution of with weight – what you need when one quantity multiplies another and you want the result's histogram. Convolving a distance distribution with a distance-ratio distribution is exactly this: every times every ratio, weighted by both.

Done without forming the outer product. That matters at the sizes this is reached with: the array version allocates twice, once for the products and once for the weights, purely to hand them to a histogram.

Bin edges are uniform from lo to hi. Values outside are dropped; the top edge is closed, so a value exactly at hi lands in the last bin rather than nowhere.
